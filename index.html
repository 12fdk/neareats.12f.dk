Features

The bit other map apps leave you guessing

A pin on a map tells you a restaurant exists. It doesn't tell you whether it's open, whether they can feed you, or whether you can get through the door.

Open now — or closing in 25 minutes

Not just today's hours. NearEats reads the clock and tells you what's actually open, and warns you when a place is about to shut before you walk there.

Two map sources, one clean list

OpenStreetMap and Apple Maps, merged. A place that exists in both appears once, keeping the best details from each — broad coverage without seeing everything twice.

Vegan, halal, kosher, gluten-free

Diet options are on the card, not buried three taps down — so you know before you walk over whether there is anything on the menu for you.

Step-free access, up front

Wheelchair access and accessible toilets are shown on the venue, alongside the honest negatives — a red badge when a place is not accessible, so it never wastes your trip.

Can't decide? Tap Surprise me

One tap picks a place for you and drops you on it. The cure for standing on a corner with three hungry people and no decision.

Cash-only warnings

The gotchas other directories bury: cash only, no card, no step-free entrance. Better to know from the pavement than at the till.

How it works

Three steps, and none of them is signing up.

1 Open it

Everything nearby, straight away

No account, no sign-up, no onboarding to sit through. Allow location and the map fills with what's around you — in your home city or one you landed in an hour ago.

2 Narrow it down

Rule places out before you walk

Switch between restaurants, cafes and bars. Filter to open now, or open late. Check the diet, access and amenity badges — the reasons you'd cross a place off.

3 Go

Directions, one tap away

Pick your place and NearEats hands you turn-by-turn directions, the website and the phone number. Then it gets out of the way.
