What is NearEats?

NearEats is an iPhone app that finds restaurants, cafes and bars near you, and shows what actually decides where you go: whether a place is open right now, whether it does vegan, halal, kosher or gluten-free, whether you can get in without steps, how long the walk really is, and whether it takes cards. It costs $1.99 once, needs no account, and works in any city.

How do I find restaurants near me?

Open NearEats and allow location access. It shows every restaurant, cafe and bar around you on a map and a synced list, sorted by distance, with the opening status and walking time on each card. There is no search to type and no account to create.

How do I find vegan or halal food near me?

Diet options appear as badges on the venue card itself — vegan, vegetarian, halal, kosher, gluten-free, dairy-free and organic — so you can rule places out from the list rather than reading a menu at the door.

How do I find somewhere open late near me?

Tap the open-late filter. It narrows the list to venues still serving, and each card shows whether a place is open now or closing shortly, checked against the current time rather than a printed timetable.

How it works

Three steps, and none of them is signing up.

  1. 1 Open it

    Everything nearby, straight away

    No account, no sign-up, no onboarding to sit through. Allow location and the map fills with what's around you — in your home city or one you landed in an hour ago.

  2. 2 Narrow it down

    Rule places out before you walk

    Switch between restaurants, cafes and bars. Filter to open now, or open late. Check the diet, access and amenity badges — the reasons you'd cross a place off.

  3. 3 Go

    Directions, one tap away

    Pick your place and NearEats hands you turn-by-turn directions, the website and the phone number. Then it gets out of the way.

FAQ

Fair questions

Why does it cost anything, if it's built on open map data?

Because the alternative is worse. An app like this pays for itself either by charging you once, or by selling ads, selling your location, or charging restaurants for placement. We picked the first one. $1.99 buys the whole app, forever, and nobody has any reason to reorder your list.

Where does the information come from?

OpenStreetMap and Apple Maps. NearEats queries both and merges them, so a venue that exists in both sources appears once, keeping the best details from each. OpenStreetMap is where the rich stuff lives — opening hours, diet options, wheelchair access, amenities — because real people map it.

Why are there no reviews, ratings or photos?

Because no open data source provides them honestly, and we would rather show you nothing than show you something invented. NearEats is a directory, not a review platform: it tells you the facts about a place — hours, diet, access, amenities, awards, how far it is — and leaves the judgement to you.

What happens to my location?

It stays on your phone. There is no account and no NearEats server holding your places, and your saved and visited venues never leave the device. To find what's nearby the app sends a coordinate and a radius to OpenStreetMap and Apple Maps, as any map app must. Anonymous analytics go to PostHog, set out in the privacy policy.

Does it work abroad?

Yes — that's the case it was built for. There is nothing to set up on arrival and no region to pick; it works wherever OpenStreetMap and Apple Maps have coverage, which is most places you'd land. The app itself is available in 49 languages.

Is it just restaurants?

No — restaurants, cafes and bars, switched with a single control. Cafes cover the coffee-and-Wi-Fi question, bars the evening one, including microbreweries, craft-beer places and the venues that stay open late. Each category carries the same badges, so diet and access information is there whichever you browse.

How accurate are the opening hours?

As good as the map data, which is usually very good and occasionally stale — a venue can change its hours faster than anyone updates the map. NearEats shows what the data says, read against the current time, and names the source. For a special trip, the venue's phone number is one tap away.

What do I need to run it?

An iPhone running iOS 18 or later, and location access so it knows what "nearby" means. That is the lot: no account, no sign-up, no subscription and no ongoing cost. The app is a one-time $1.99 purchase and works in any city with map coverage, in 49 languages.
